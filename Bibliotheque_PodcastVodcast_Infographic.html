Dispatch 01
The inflection point
Reuters Advertising · EMEA Intelligence

Podcasting is no longer a channel. It is a content genre.

The format has crossed from niche to mainstream. Cultural moments now begin in podcasts and ripple outward. Appearances that once went to late-night television go to podcasts first. The intimate, long-form format lets decision-makers, analysts and business leaders speak at length — exactly the audience Reuters clients want to reach.

One billion people watch podcasts on YouTube every month. The definition of "podcast" has expanded to include video, live events, social clips and exclusive communities. Brands can now reach B2B audiences at every point in their day — commute, desk, gym, screen.

Forrester Research · The Podcast Advertising Inflection Point · December 2025

"Podcast is social, podcast is in real life, podcast is video, and podcast is certainly still audio." Consumers think of podcasters as a very particular type of content creator.

Dispatch 02
Attention & trust
Reuters Advertising · EMEA Intelligence

The medium
no one skips.

Podcasts are sought, not served. Every press of play is deliberate. That intentionality transforms the relationship between listener and the ads inside it. The brain works harder to construct audio narrative — and when it works harder, it retains more.

For B2B advertisers, this is decisive. These are business decision-makers consuming content in active, focused states — not passive scroll. Their attention is present. Their trust is earnable.

Academic validation · peer-reviewed research

Experimental research (N=330) confirms that parasocial relationships with podcast hosts positively influence brand attitudes and purchase behaviours. Listeners who feel a genuine connection to a host lower their critical guard toward ads — meaning host-read endorsements land as trusted personal recommendations, not interruptions. This effect is unique to podcasting and cannot be replicated through display or social placements.

Dispatch 04
The UK opportunity
Reuters Advertising · EMEA Intelligence

The UK audience
already decided.
The spend hasn't.

43% monthly reach. 79% past-week ad recall. Yet podcasts attract less than 5% of all UK audio advertising spend. This is not a demand problem — it is a buyer awareness problem. The infrastructure exists. The audience is habitual.

Adjusted for audience size, the UK spends one-fifth of what the US does per listener-hour. That is 5.2× headroom against a market that took eight years to grow 7×.

For context: Nielsen's global benchmark for podcast aided recall sits at 71% — already the highest of any emerging media channel. The UK's 79% surpasses even that. This audience is not just present. It is paying attention.

Recommendations

What this means
for your clients.

01

Present podcast and vodcast as a primary channel recommendation for B2B clients targeting decision-makers — not an add-on or experiment.

02

Lead with 2× TV attention and #1 trust across all legacy media. These numbers reframe the conversation from niche to necessity.

03

For finance and professional services clients, flag Business & Money (+29) and News (+24) as under-supplied at the 35–54 index. First movers build positions competitors can't buy back.

04

The video extension argument is now data-backed. Adding vodcast to audio increases reach by 39%. Clients who resist audio may still commit to video-first.

05

Use the UK spend paradox as urgency: 43% reach, 79% recall, under 5% of audio spend. This market compresses quickly — early movers lock in share of voice.

06

Reuters' B2B decision-maker audience maps directly onto the three over-indexed UK genres. The Reuters context adds a trust halo no algorithmic placement can replicate.
