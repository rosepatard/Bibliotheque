Dispatch 01
Our position
The argument
Not every campaign needs translation.
But some absolutely do.

For global campaigns, English as a lingua franca makes sense. But when a client wants to reach a specific non-English speaking market — France, Germany, Japan, the Middle East, Latin America — serving English-only content is an active performance handicap. The data is unambiguous.

Global campaigns
English as lingua franca

Multi-market campaigns across mixed-language environments. English-first is appropriate — broad reach where no single language dominates.

Standard practice ✓
Market-specific campaigns → our focus
Translation as the default

A client targeting France, Germany, the Middle East, or Japan. English-only creative enters a local-language environment where it will underperform — sometimes dramatically.

Finding
CSA Research B2B, 2020 · n=956 · 24 countries
Preference data
81 out of 100 B2B buyers prefer to purchase in their own language.

This isn't a marginal preference. Across 24 countries, business buyers overwhelmingly chose native-language content — even those who rated their English proficiency as high. Fluency and preference are not the same thing.

Recommendations
For discussion · EMEA leadership · June 2026 Bibliothèque · Ref 001 · Internal
The attention is there.
The language is the barrier.
01

When a campaign targets a specific non-English speaking market, translation should be the recommended default — not an optional extra.

02

Work with Product to build a scalable AI-assisted, human-reviewed translation workflow priced into market-specific packages from the outset.

03

Reuters.com/ar is already a natural test bed. Arabic-language ads on that site would be a direct, measurable experiment we can run now.

04

Equip sales teams globally with this evidence. The IDA Ireland and Arabic site data belong in every relevant client conversation.

05

Audit existing market-specific campaigns running English-only. Where are we capping client performance by not recommending translation?

06

Frame translation as a performance line item, not a cost. 67% of B2B buyers pay up to 30% more for localised solutions. The evidence makes the case.
